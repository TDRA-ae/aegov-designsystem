# Content guideline

Write in a clear, professional-yet-approachable government voice using plain British English, sentence case, and accessible phrasing.

## Rules

- **Voice:** corporate yet approachable — professional, accessible, user-centric, clear, confident.
- **Tone:** short, meaningful messages; clear next-step directions; humble, positive reinforcement instead of blame; offer help to prevent errors.
- Use **first person:** "we" for the entity, "you" for the user.
- Avoid technical jargon; use simple, human-readable language and accessible alternatives to intimidating terms.
- Spell out acronyms at first mention with the acronym in parentheses, unless commonly known.
- Minimize contractions — prefer "cannot" over "can't".
- Avoid witty, sarcastic, or playful language; use idioms sparingly and only if widely understood; be direct and specific, not vague.
- **British English** (Oxford dictionary preferred).
- **Sentence case only** — capitalize the first word and proper nouns ("Learn more," not "Learn More").
- Use the **Oxford/serial comma** for lists of three or more items.
- Single quotation marks for direct speech, double for nested quotations.
- Exclamation points only when required for corporate voice; avoid overuse.
- Semicolons sparingly (prefer em dashes or splitting sentences); unspaced hyphen for phrases, unspaced en dash for ranges, unspaced em dash for elaboration.
- Use "and" not "&" unless part of a brand name.
- Spell out numbers at the start of a sentence; use numerals within sentences; add commas to figures over 3 digits (1,000; 200,000).
- Limit boldface to 1–2 instances per paragraph; italics for emphasis/citations/definitions, not for hyperlinks.
- **Formal government navigation labels:** "Services" (not "What we do"), "About" (not "Who we are"), "Contact" (not "Get in touch"), "Frequently asked questions" (not "FAQs").
- Do not use "e-services"/"eservice" — all services are electronic.
- **Accessibility:** sentence case for screen-reader compatibility (no title case, camelCase, or ALL-CAPS); avoid Unicode characters screen readers misread; descriptive language ("watch video" not "play video"); accurate alt text; transcripts for audio, captions + transcripts for video.

## Common violations to flag

- Title Case in headings, buttons, or labels (should be sentence case).
- American spellings instead of British/Oxford.
- Jargon, acronyms without first-mention expansion, or "e-services"/"eservice".
- Informal nav labels ("Get in touch", "FAQs", "Who we are").
- Missing Oxford comma; overused exclamation points; contractions like "can't".
- Numbers not comma-formatted over 3 digits, or numerals starting a sentence.
- ALL-CAPS / camelCase text, missing alt text, or video/audio without transcripts/captions.
- Blaming or vague error copy without a clear next step.
